This week I stumbled upon "The Worst Person In The Word" directed by Joachim Trier. I never followed his filmography before and this is my first Trier fil and what a first impression it left.

On paper, it's a Norwegian romantic comedy-drama that follows four years in the life of Julie, an ambitious millennial girl trying to figure out who she is, who she wants to be with, and what she is even supposed to do with her life. Along the way she runs into an identity crisis, complicated relationships, and a family that never quite held together. But "on paper" doesn't do it justice. It's the kind of movie that sits quietly with you long after it ends.

## The frames

I'm a Wong Kar-wai person. Give me longing, give me people drifting through beautiful spaces without knowing what they want, and I'm sold. Oslo is not Hong Kong, but this movie made me feel that same ache. I was awestruck by the cinematography. Every other shot felt like it deserved to be paused, screenshotted, and framed.

## Cheating, but make it surreal

I have seen cheating shown in cinema in a hundred ways , usually as a betrayal, scandal ,a plot device. Here it feels almost dreamlike. When julie met Eivind, nothing happens and yet a lot happens.
There is a stretch where two of them play at the edge of intimacy without technically crossing the line and somehow thats more charged any kiss or sex scene.

Than comes the scene where time itself freezes Julie runs through a stopped city to get to him and its pure surrealism.

## What stayed with me...

To be honest I am struggling to put it into words and maybe that's the point of movie. I loved each and every frame of this movie , sometimes its funny than its devastating , than again its funny and than it ends.

Coming from India, where a woman's ambitions, nuances and vulnerabilities are so often judged through preconceived notions, I found it refreshing to watch a film that is driven entirely by a woman and lets her convey her emotions so naturally. A woman who is ambitious, who changes her mind about her career, who walks out of one relationship and into another, doesn't usually get to be "figuring it out" here. She gets called confused, or selfish, or worse. Julie gets to be messy, vulnerable, wrong, and still be the center of her own story. I'm not saying it's impossible in our cinema, but I rarely see it, and never this honestly.
